AI is no longer just a tool we use — it’s code we *run*. From auto-reply features to smart content generation, builders are embedding AI models directly into their applications. If you’ve worked with AI before, you can probably relate — and if not, take my word for it: things can go sideways fast.

This article shares the five key safeguards I follow when integrating AI into real-world systems, particularly when user inputs are involved or when the AI’s output triggers further logic or actions.

---

## 1. Validate All User Input

If your AI takes in user input, assume it will be manipulated.

**Why it matters:**  
Prompt injection is not theoretical—it’s already exploited in production apps. Malicious users can insert prompts that leak internal instructions or make the model behave unpredictably.

**Defensive steps:**
- Escape special characters or formatting
- Strip out `<script>` tags or command-like text
- Enforce strict length and structure with tools like [Pydantic](https://docs.pydantic.dev/) (Python) or [Zod](https://zod.dev/) (TypeScript)
- Use allowlists where applicable

> 🔐 Assume any input to your AI could be trying to jailbreak it.

---

## 2. Verify AI Output Before Acting on It

It’s tempting to treat AI output as truth, especially when it sounds convincing. But don’t.

**Real dangers include:**
- Executing shell commands generated by AI without checks
- Writing incorrect or insecure config files
- Making business decisions based on hallucinated data

**Best practices:**
- Check for unsafe patterns in text/code
- Use confidence filters, keyword whitelists, or output validation
- Require human approval for sensitive actions

> ⚠️ Treat AI like a smart intern — useful, but not infallible.

## 5. Build for Failure

Your AI endpoint will fail. The model will hallucinate. You must plan for it.

**What you need:**
- Timeout logic and retries with exponential backoff
- Fallback messages like “Sorry, I didn’t catch that—please try again.”
- Graceful degradation if the AI service is offline
- Rate limiting to avoid overloading your API keys

**Bonus:** Add a “confidence threshold” filter. Only let the AI act if its output passes a sanity check.

> 🧯 AI should enhance the UX, not become a single point of failure.

## Final Thoughts

The future of software is AI-enhanced — but it must also be secure, stable, and trustworthy. If you’re embedding AI into your tools or workflows, you’re not just writing prompts — you’re shaping system behavior. That means thinking like a builder, a product owner, and a risk manager all at once.
